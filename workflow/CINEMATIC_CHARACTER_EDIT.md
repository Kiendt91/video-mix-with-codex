# Cinematic Character Edit

This mode is for edits like a serious character tribute, not a beat-cut montage. The target is an emotional arc built from dialogue, music, close-ups, wide shots, and restraint.

## Core Difference

Beat-cut montage:

- music drives the edit
- cuts land on beats
- visual energy is the main structure

Cinematic character edit:

- dialogue and character arc drive the edit
- music supports the emotional rise
- cuts often land on sentence endings, glances, scene reversals, and score swells
- transitions are mostly invisible

## Default Format

- 3840x1584 or 1920x792
- 24fps
- 2.42:1 ultrawide frame
- dark/cool grade with controlled highlights
- subtle grain and vignette
- subtitle text centered near the bottom
- minimal title card

## Story Arc Template

1. **Cold Open**: quiet establishing shot or isolated close-up.
2. **Confession**: first line that defines the wound, fear, or contradiction.
3. **Mask**: show the public persona, strategy, mission, or lie.
4. **Cost**: reveal what the character gives up.
5. **Choice**: the decisive line or visual turn.
6. **Climax**: strongest music and largest visual scale.
7. **Aftermath**: hold on face, empty room, object, or final line.

## Shot Selection Rules

- Prefer eyes, hands, silhouettes, vehicles leaving frame, doors, corridors, reflections, and wide negative space.
- Do not overuse action shots. Use them as punctuation.
- Keep dialogue shots readable. The viewer must know who is speaking emotionally, not just technically.
- Avoid source subtitles burned into footage unless they are intentionally part of the look.
- Keep repeated locations purposeful: setup, callback, consequence.

## Audio Rules

- Dialogue must be clear before the music sounds impressive.
- Use music ducking by lowering the bed under dialogue or by using quieter sections of the track.
- Do not stack too many dialogue lines over dense music.
- Leave short silence or near-silence before major emotional lines.

## Subtitle Rules

- Use one or two lines maximum.
- Do not caption every sound; caption only the line that matters.
- Keep subtitles on screen long enough to read.
- Use restrained typography: 30-44px at 3840 width, white/off-white, slight shadow.

## QA Additions

In addition to normal black-frame and render checks:

- verify no subtitle cue overlaps the wrong speaker
- verify dialogue is audible over music
- verify each section advances the character arc
- extract frames at every major dialogue line
- inspect the final 20% of the video closely; this is where cinematic edits usually peak
